The most expensive bugs are the ones in your assumptions. Before a project starts,
a little clarity saves months of rework. Here's the checklist we walk through with
every new client.

## Before you build

- **The problem, in one sentence.** If you can't state it simply, the scope isn't
  clear yet.
- **Who it's for.** The primary user and their single most important job.
- **Success metrics.** How you'll know the software worked — concrete and
  measurable.
- **Must-have vs. nice-to-have.** Ruthlessly separate the two for v1.

## Scope and delivery

1. Agree the first milestone — small enough to ship quickly.
2. Decide the engagement model (fixed scope, dedicated team, or partnership).
3. Set a cadence for demos and feedback.
4. Name a single decision-maker on each side.

## The technical foundations

- Hosting and infrastructure (we default to scalable, secure AWS).
- Data model and integrations with existing systems.
- Security, access control and compliance needs.
- A plan for documentation and handover from day one.

## After launch

Software isn't done at launch — it's maintained. Decide up front who handles
updates, monitoring and support, so nothing falls through the cracks.

> A good kickoff replaces opinions with decisions. Every decision you make now is
> one you won't argue about mid-build.
