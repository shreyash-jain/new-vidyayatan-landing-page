## What the Conclave is

Presented by [IIRF](https://iirfranking.com/), the International Institutional Ranking Framework, the ICOSA School Conclave 2026 follows the first ICOSA Conclave and Awards, held at the same venue on 11 December 2025. This year's theme is *Beyond Accreditation, Towards Transformation*, and it brings school leaders, policymakers and academics together for a day.

That theme reflects how ICOSA sees its role. Accreditation is treated as the beginning of something rather than the end of it. Its evaluators are working educators, and they hand a school a plan for getting better. Its benchmarks are international, and they are designed to sit alongside a school's own character and traditions rather than replace them.

## What ICOSA measures

ICOSA publishes its framework in full. Every school is assessed twice over: once by an ICOSA expert panel, and once by the school itself. Both use a 5-point scale, and **the final grade is the average of the two**. Six areas carry 100 marks each — academic performance, teacher and teaching competence, teaching resources and pedagogy, sports and infrastructure, 21st-century skills, and innovative curriculum. Grades run from Foundational (2.0–2.50) up to Excellence (3.51–5.0).

Because the school scores itself alongside the panel, the process doubles as a structured self-review. Comparing the two perspectives is often where the most useful insight sits — it shows a school exactly where its own view and an expert view line up, and where there is room to grow. The whole thing happens online, takes 7 to 10 days, and ends with a digital certificate that a parent can verify by scanning a QR code, plus a listing in ICOSA's public directory. Schools in more than 15 countries have been through it.

## The AI-Enabled School Readiness track

ICOSA has also launched a second, separate accreditation: **AI-Enabled School Readiness**, which it calls a first of its kind. Forty-eight questions across six areas — infrastructure, curriculum, faculty, student outcomes, governance and innovation — produce a score ICOSA calls the AI Index, marked out of 100. Schools have 30 days to complete it, and are recognised as an AI-Enabled School at 2.5 out of 5 or above.

![ICOSA's AI-Enabled School Readiness Accreditation: forty-eight assessment questions across six dimensions — infrastructure, curriculum, faculty, student outcomes, governance and innovation — producing a 0 to 100 ICOSA AI Index, with recognition at 2.5 out of 5 or above](/images/blog/icosa-ai-readiness.svg)

This is the part we find most interesting. Measuring AI readiness across governance and student outcomes — not just equipment — gives a school a clear, structured picture of where it actually stands and what to do next. It also brings the practical questions into focus: how AI is used in the classroom, [how a child's work is looked after when it sits inside a supplier's system](/blog/dpdp-act-schools-edtech-compliance), and how a school can tell that a tool is genuinely helping. Those are the questions we work on every week, which is a large part of why this partnership makes sense.
