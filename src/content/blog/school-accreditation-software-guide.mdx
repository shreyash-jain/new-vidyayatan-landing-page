Parents compare schools online long before they visit one, and a framed certificate on a principal's wall proves nothing to someone reading on a phone. That is the gap platforms like [ICOSA](https://icosaonline.com/) close — a structured way for a school to demonstrate quality, and for a parent to verify it in seconds.

If you're an education group, a franchise network, or an accreditation body, here's what the build involves. *ICOSA appears throughout as an industry example, not as a Vidyayatan client project.*

## Start with the framework, not the features

ICOSA's published methodology is a useful reference: the arithmetic is stated openly. Six pillars, each worth 100 marks: academic performance, teaching competence, resources and pedagogy, sports and infrastructure, 21st-century skills, innovative curriculum. Two parallel evaluations, an expert panel score and a school self-assessment, each on a 5-point scale, with **the final grade being the average of the two**. Bands run from Foundational (2.0–2.50) to Excellence (3.51–5.0); the process is online, 7–10 days.

![How the grade is computed: six pillars of 100 marks each, scored once by an expert panel and once by the school, averaged on a 5-point scale into bands from Foundational to Excellence](/images/blog/school-accreditation-scoring-model.svg)

Read that as an engineer and one line dominates: the applicant controls half of its own grade. That is a transparent, defensible methodology — and it dictates the data model, where these builds quietly succeed or fail.

## The modules that matter

**Application and payment workflow.** Registration, fees, document upload, a status dashboard. The easy part — and the one most estimates over-weight.

**The scoring engine.** Store every criterion score as its own row carrying the rubric version, the evidence it was scored against, the scorer's identity, and a timestamp. The final grade is *derived*, always. Two rules follow. Rubric versions are immutable: a weighting change creates v3, never edits v2, or a 2026 certificate becomes unexplainable in 2029. And store the **divergence** between self-score and expert score per criterion — a school rating itself 1.8 points above the panel on faculty isn't necessarily dishonest, but it's the file a reviewer opens first.

**Evidence handling.** This is where the cost lands. Hash every upload and keep it immutable, so an appeal is argued against the exact bytes that were scored. It is also **children's data**: [Section 9 of India's DPDP Act, 2023](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/98869575/) requires verifiable parental consent and bans tracking, profiling and targeted advertising aimed at children — prohibitions consent does not unlock. The Rules were [notified in November 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2190014), phased in over eighteen months. The cheapest fix is rubric design, not a consent modal: ask for "percentage of the cohort above 90" instead of "upload the mark sheets" and a whole compliance surface disappears.

**AI assistance.** Three places it earns its keep: extracting figures from long PDFs with the page cited, flagging contradictions (the staff list says 62 teachers, the claimed ratio implies 45), and detecting reviewer drift across a cohort. Not in issuing the grade. A school that paid a fee and got A instead of A++ will ask why. *"The panel scored it, here is the criterion and the evidence"* is an answer. *"The model scored it"* is not.

**Certificate and QR verification.** Treat this as an API, not a PDF. Opaque credential IDs, validity evaluated server-side on every scan, and **revocation as a first-class feature** — a QR that returns "valid" forever is a liability the day an accredited school has an incident. The [W3C Verifiable Credentials 2.0 standard, a Recommendation since 15 May 2025](https://www.w3.org/press-releases/2025/verifiable-credentials-2-0/), gives you a portable format and a revocation model you don't have to invent — though almost nobody in K-12 asks for it yet, and a signed payload plus a hosted status endpoint delivers most of the value.

**Public directory.** Usually the highest-traffic page and the best organic search asset the body will own. Pre-render it rather than hitting the transactional database per request, and keep each school's URL stable across renewals.

## Recommended stack

![Architecture: applicant workflow, assessor workbench and public verification sit above a shared scoring engine, writing to Postgres, hashed object storage and an append-only audit log, with a cached read layer serving the directory and QR lookups](/images/blog/school-accreditation-architecture.svg)

- **Next.js** for the public site, directory and verification — SEO and caching come with the rendering model.
- **Node or Python services**, with the scoring engine as a pure function over `(rubric_version, criterion_scores)`: testable, replayable, explainable without a debugger.
- **PostgreSQL** for scores, rubrics and audit; object storage for evidence; the public read layer kept separate so admissions-season traffic can't contend with reviewers mid-assessment.
- **Multi-currency payments** decided up front if international schools are in scope — currency is a schema decision, not a plugin.
- **An append-only audit log from day one.** Retrofitting audit after certificates have been issued is the most expensive rework in this category.

## Timeline and cost

An MVP — self-assessment, single-track scoring, a generated certificate — runs **8–12 weeks**. The full platform with dual assessment, a reviewer panel, payments, the directory and QR verification is **4–6 months**. Add AI-assisted evaluation, appeals, revocation and multi-country support and it is **6–9 months**. These are our estimating ranges from comparable builds, not published benchmarks.

![Delivery ranges: an MVP takes 8 to 12 weeks, a full platform 4 to 6 months, and an advanced platform 6 to 9 months](/images/blog/school-accreditation-timeline.svg)

What moves those numbers, in order: **how often the rubric changes**, how many programmes share one platform, whether reviewers work offline, and whether appeals are in scope.

One honest caveat: below roughly 50 institutions a cycle, don't build this. Forms, a spreadsheet and one hosted verification page will run the process while you're still learning it. Build when the rubric has stopped changing and reviewer time, not software, is the bottleneck — the same [judgement we worked through with HABUILD](/blog/habuild-build-vs-buy).
