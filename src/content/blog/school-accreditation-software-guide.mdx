Parents compare schools online long before they visit one, and a framed certificate on a principal's wall proves nothing to someone reading on a phone. That is the gap platforms like [ICOSA](https://icosaonline.com/) close: a structured way for a school to demonstrate quality and for a parent to verify it in seconds. ICOSA frames its own goal as **the Borderless Graduate**: a future where education is a universal passport, and schools produce students who are academically astute, culturally fluent, and hold the global command to lead, innovate and thrive internationally. **Vidyayatan builds this category of platform end to end** — framework design, scoring engine, evidence handling, AI-assisted evaluation, public verification. Here is what that build involves. *ICOSA appears throughout as a published industry example, not as a Vidyayatan client project.*

## Start with the framework, not the features

ICOSA's published methodology is a useful reference — the arithmetic is stated openly. Six pillars, 100 marks each: academic performance, teaching competence, resources and pedagogy, sports and infrastructure, 21st-century skills, innovative curriculum. Two parallel evaluations, an expert panel score and a school self-assessment, each on a 5-point scale, with **the final grade being the average of the two**. Bands run from Foundational (2.0–2.50) to Excellence (3.51–5.0), online, in 7–10 days.

Read that as an engineer and one line dominates: the applicant controls half of its own grade. Transparent and defensible — and it dictates the data model, where these builds quietly succeed or fail.

## The vision the software serves

That vision is a catalyst, not a regulatory hurdle — continuous improvement past the certificate, evaluators who are practising educators handing back a growth roadmap rather than an audit report, and international benchmarks that don't erase a school's own heritage.

Treat that as a product requirement, not a mission statement: a platform that only issues grades delivers none of it. **A roadmap needs per-criterion scores held against a stable rubric version**, so this cycle can be compared with the last. Local identity needs context travelling alongside the score, not averaged out of it. Keeping pace with 2030 means new pillars must be addable without invalidating certificates already issued. Design for the second cycle, not the first.

## The modules that matter

**Application and payment workflow.** Registration, fees, document upload, status dashboard. The easy part — and the one most estimates over-weight.

**The scoring engine.** Store every criterion score as its own row: rubric version, the evidence scored, the scorer, a timestamp. The final grade is *derived*, always. Rubric versions are immutable: a weighting change creates v3, never edits v2. And store the **divergence** between self-score and expert score per criterion: a school rating itself 1.8 points above the panel isn't necessarily dishonest, but it's the file a reviewer opens first.

**Evidence handling.** This is where the cost lands. Hash every upload and keep it immutable, so an appeal is argued against the exact bytes scored. It is also **children's data**: [Section 9 of India's DPDP Act, 2023](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/98869575/) requires verifiable parental consent and bans tracking, profiling and targeted ads aimed at children — prohibitions consent does not unlock. The Rules followed in [November 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2190014). The cheapest fix is rubric design, not a consent modal: ask for "percentage of the cohort above 90" rather than "upload the mark sheets" and a compliance surface disappears.

**AI assistance.** Three places it earns its keep: extracting figures from long PDFs with the page cited, flagging contradictions, spotting reviewer drift across a cohort. Not in issuing the grade. A school that paid a fee and got A instead of A++ will ask why. *"The panel scored it, here is the criterion and the evidence"* is an answer. *"The model scored it"* is not.

**Certificate and QR verification.** Treat this as an API, not a PDF. Opaque credential IDs, validity evaluated server-side on every scan, and **revocation as a first-class feature** — a QR that returns "valid" forever is a liability the day a school has an incident. The [W3C Verifiable Credentials 2.0 standard](https://www.w3.org/press-releases/2025/verifiable-credentials-2-0/), a Recommendation since 15 May 2025, gives a portable format and a ready-made revocation model.

**Public directory.** Usually the highest-traffic page and the best search asset the body will own. Pre-render it; keep each school's URL stable across renewals.

## Recommended stack

![Architecture: applicant workflow, assessor workbench and public verification sit above a shared scoring engine, writing to Postgres, hashed object storage and an append-only audit log, with a cached read layer serving the directory and QR lookups](/images/blog/school-accreditation-architecture.svg)

- **Next.js** for the public site, directory and verification — SEO and caching come with the rendering model.
- **Node or Python services**, the scoring engine a pure function over `(rubric_version, criterion_scores)`: testable, replayable, explainable.
- **PostgreSQL** for scores, rubrics and audit; object storage for evidence; the public read layer kept off the transactional core.
- **Multi-currency payments** decided up front if international schools are in scope — currency is a schema decision, not a plugin.
- **An append-only audit log from day one.** Retrofitting audit after certificates exist is the most expensive rework here.

## Timeline and cost

An MVP — self-assessment, one scoring track, a certificate — runs **8–12 weeks**. The full platform — dual assessment, reviewer panel, payments, directory, QR verification — is **4–6 months**; with AI-assisted evaluation, appeals and multi-country support, **6–9 months**. Our estimating ranges, not published benchmarks.

![Delivery ranges: an MVP takes 8 to 12 weeks, a full platform 4 to 6 months, and an advanced platform 6 to 9 months](/images/blog/school-accreditation-timeline.svg)

One honest caveat: below roughly 50 institutions a cycle, don't build this. Forms, a spreadsheet and one hosted verification page will run the process while you learn it. Build when the rubric has settled and reviewer time, not software, is the bottleneck — the [judgement we worked through with HABUILD](/blog/habuild-build-vs-buy).
